## Introduction

Protein is the most critical nutrient for building and maintaining muscle. Whether you are trying to gain muscle, lose fat while preserving lean mass, or simply eat a more balanced diet, knowing which foods pack the most protein per calorie can transform your results.

This list covers 50 of the best high-protein foods across every category — meat, fish, dairy, eggs, legumes, and plant-based options. Each entry includes the protein content per 100g so you can compare and build meals that hit your daily targets.

## How Much Protein Do You Need?

Before diving into the list, here is a quick reference for daily protein targets:

- **Sedentary adults:** 0.8g per kg of body weight (minimum)
- **Recreational exercisers:** 1.2-1.6g per kg
- **Muscle building / strength training:** 1.6-2.2g per kg
- **Fat loss with exercise:** 1.8-2.4g per kg

For an 80kg person aiming to build muscle, that translates to 128-176g of protein per day.

## Meat and Poultry

### 1. [Chicken Breast](/chicken-breast) — 31g protein per 100g
The gold standard of lean protein. Skinless, boneless chicken breast has just 165 calories per 100g, making it one of the most efficient protein sources available.

### 2. [Turkey Breast](/turkey-breast) — 29g protein per 100g
Nearly as lean as chicken breast with a slightly different flavor profile. Excellent sliced for sandwiches or ground for burgers and meatballs.

### 3. Lean [Ground Beef](/ground-beef) (95% lean) — 26g protein per 100g
Leaner cuts of ground beef deliver strong protein with less fat. Great for tacos, bolognese, and stir-fries.

### 4. [Beef](/beef) Sirloin — 27g protein per 100g
A flavorful steak cut that balances protein with moderate fat. Grilled or pan-seared, it makes a satisfying centerpiece for any meal.

### 5. Pork Tenderloin — 26g protein per 100g
Often overlooked, pork tenderloin is nearly as lean as chicken breast. It is tender, mild-flavored, and pairs well with a variety of seasonings.

### 6. [Lamb](/lamb) Leg — 25g protein per 100g
A good protein source that is popular worldwide. Roasted lamb leg provides excellent protein along with iron and B vitamins.

### 7. Bison — 28g protein per 100g
Leaner than beef with a similar taste. Bison has become increasingly available and is an excellent alternative red meat.

### 8. Venison — 30g protein per 100g
One of the leanest red meats available. Venison is high in protein and iron with minimal fat.

### 9. Chicken Thigh (skinless) — 26g protein per 100g
More flavorful and juicier than breast, with slightly more fat. Still an excellent protein source and often more affordable.

### 10. Duck Breast (skinless) — 23g protein per 100g
Rich and flavorful. Removing the skin significantly reduces the fat content while keeping the distinctive taste.

## Fish and Seafood

### 11. [Tuna](/tuna) (canned in water) — 26g protein per 100g
One of the most convenient and affordable protein sources. Keep cans in your pantry for quick salads and wraps.

### 12. [Salmon](/salmon) — 20g protein per 100g
Delivers high-quality protein plus omega-3 fatty acids for heart and brain health. Aim for 2-3 servings per week.

### 13. [Shrimp](/shrimp) — 24g protein per 100g
Extremely lean with only 99 calories per 100g. Shrimp cooks quickly and works in everything from stir-fries to salads.

### 14. Cod — 18g protein per 100g
A mild white fish with very few calories (82 per 100g). Perfect for people who prefer a less fishy taste.

### 15. Tilapia — 26g protein per 100g
Affordable, mild-flavored, and widely available. Tilapia is a practical everyday fish for hitting protein goals.

### 16. Sardines — 25g protein per 100g
Packed with omega-3s and calcium (from the soft bones). Sardines are nutritional powerhouses despite their small size.

### 17. Halibut — 23g protein per 100g
A meaty, mild fish that holds up well to grilling and baking. Excellent protein-to-calorie ratio.

### 18. Mahi Mahi — 24g protein per 100g
Firm-textured and flavorful with just 85 calories per 100g. A great choice for fish tacos and grilled dishes.

### 19. Scallops — 21g protein per 100g
Low-calorie and high-protein with a sweet, delicate flavor. Seared scallops are a quick, elegant protein source.

### 20. Anchovies — 29g protein per 100g
Intensely flavored and rich in omega-3s. Add to pasta sauces, salads, or pizza for a protein and flavor boost.

## Dairy and Eggs

### 21. [Eggs](/eggs) — 13g protein per 100g (6g per large egg)
The original complete protein. Eggs contain all essential amino acids and are incredibly versatile. Do not skip the yolk — it contains nearly half the protein plus essential nutrients.

### 22. Greek Yogurt (nonfat) — 10g protein per 100g
Strained yogurt contains roughly double the protein of regular yogurt. A 200g serving delivers 20g of protein for about 130 calories.

### 23. Cottage Cheese (low-fat) — 11g protein per 100g
A bodybuilder staple for good reason. Cottage cheese is high in casein protein, which digests slowly and is ideal before bed.

### 24. Parmesan Cheese — 35g protein per 100g
The highest protein cheese. Even a small 20g grating adds 7g of protein to pasta, salads, and soups.

### 25. Swiss Cheese — 27g protein per 100g
One of the higher-protein cheeses with a distinctive nutty flavor.

### 26. Skyr — 11g protein per 100g
This Icelandic dairy product is similar to Greek yogurt but with an even thicker texture and slightly more protein.

### 27. Whey Protein Powder — 75-80g protein per 100g
The most efficient protein source per calorie. One scoop typically provides 24-30g of protein for 100-130 calories.

## Legumes and Plant-Based

### 28. [Lentils](/lentils) (cooked) — 9g protein per 100g
A plant-based protein champion. Lentils also provide fiber and iron. A 200g serving delivers 18g of protein.

### 29. Chickpeas (cooked) — 9g protein per 100g
Versatile legumes used in [hummus](/hummus), curries, salads, and roasted as a crunchy snack.

### 30. Black Beans (cooked) — 9g protein per 100g
A staple in Latin American cuisine. Combine with [rice](/rice) for a complete amino acid profile.

### 31. [Tofu](/tofu) (firm) — 8g protein per 100g
Absorbs any flavor you cook it with. Press and bake, stir-fry, or scramble for a plant-based protein base.

### 32. Tempeh — 19g protein per 100g
Fermented soybean product with nearly double the protein of tofu plus a nutty flavor and firm texture.

### 33. Edamame — 11g protein per 100g
Young soybeans that make a great snack or salad addition. A 150g serving provides about 17g of protein.

### 34. Seitan — 25g protein per 100g
Made from wheat gluten, seitan has the highest protein content of any plant-based food. Its meaty texture makes it a popular meat substitute.

### 35. [Peanut Butter](/peanut-butter) — 25g protein per 100g
High in protein and fat. While calorie-dense, a 30g serving adds a quick 8g of protein to smoothies, toast, and oatmeal.

### 36. [Almonds](/almonds) — 21g protein per 100g
Among the highest-protein nuts. A 30g handful provides 6g of protein along with healthy fats and vitamin E.

### 37. Pumpkin Seeds — 19g protein per 100g
A nutrient-dense seed packed with protein, magnesium, and zinc. Sprinkle on salads, yogurt, or oatmeal.

### 38. Hemp Seeds — 31g protein per 100g
A complete plant protein containing all essential amino acids. Add to smoothies, salads, or [oats](/oats).

### 39. [Quinoa](/quinoa) (cooked) — 4g protein per 100g
While not as high as other foods on this list, quinoa is a complete protein grain that adds up when eaten in larger portions.

### 40. Nutritional Yeast — 50g protein per 100g
A cheesy-flavored powder popular in vegan cooking. Just 2 tablespoons add 8g of protein to any dish.

## Other High-Protein Foods

### 41. Beef Jerky — 33g protein per 100g
One of the most protein-dense snacks available. Watch sodium content and choose brands with minimal added sugar.

### 42. Turkey Jerky — 30g protein per 100g
Leaner than beef jerky with similarly impressive protein density.

### 43. Protein Bars — 20-30g protein per bar (varies)
Convenient for on-the-go protein. Check labels — many bars contain significant added sugar.

### 44. Canned Chicken — 25g protein per 100g
Shelf-stable and ready to eat. Useful for quick salads, wraps, and sandwiches when fresh chicken is unavailable.

### 45. Egg Whites — 11g protein per 100g
Pure protein with virtually zero fat. Use alongside whole [eggs](/eggs) to boost protein without adding many calories.

### 46. [Shrimp](/shrimp) (cooked) — 24g protein per 100g
Repeated from seafood because of its exceptional protein-to-calorie ratio (99 calories per 100g).

### 47. Biltong — 56g protein per 100g
A South African dried meat snack. Even higher in protein than jerky because it has less moisture.

### 48. Canned Sardines in Water — 24g protein per 100g
An underrated pantry staple. High protein, omega-3 fats, and calcium all in one affordable can.

### 49. Bone Broth — 5g protein per 100ml
Low-calorie and rich in collagen protein. Drink on its own or use as a base for soups and sauces.

### 50. Casein Protein Powder — 70-80g protein per 100g
Slow-digesting protein powder ideal for shakes before bed or when you want sustained amino acid delivery.

## How to Eat More Protein Each Day

- **Start breakfast with protein.** [Eggs](/eggs), Greek yogurt, or a protein smoothie set the tone for the day.
- **Include protein at every meal.** Aim for 30-40g per meal across 4 eating occasions.
- **Keep high-protein snacks on hand.** Greek yogurt, beef jerky, cottage cheese, and [almonds](/almonds) travel well.
- **Batch cook proteins on Sunday.** Grill a week's worth of [chicken breast](/chicken-breast) or bake [salmon](/salmon) fillets so meals come together quickly.
- **Add protein to carb-heavy meals.** Stir [eggs](/eggs) into fried [rice](/rice), add [lentils](/lentils) to soup, or top pasta with parmesan.

## Key Takeaways

- The best high-protein foods per 100g include chicken breast (31g), tuna (26g), shrimp (24g), Greek yogurt (10g), and lentils (9g)
- For muscle building, aim for 1.6-2.2g of protein per kg of body weight daily
- Distribute protein across 3-4 meals rather than loading it all into one meal
- Both animal and plant sources can meet your needs — tempeh, seitan, and legumes are excellent plant-based options
- Prioritize whole food protein sources over supplements, but use protein powder when convenience matters
