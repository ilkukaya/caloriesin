## Introduction

Fiber is one of the most underappreciated nutrients in the modern diet. Most adults eat only 15-17 grams of fiber per day, well below the recommended 25-30 grams. This gap has real consequences: poor digestion, unstable blood sugar, increased cholesterol, and difficulty managing weight.

The good news is that boosting your fiber intake is straightforward once you know which foods to prioritize. This guide covers 30 of the best high-fiber foods, explains the different types of fiber, and provides practical tips for increasing your intake without digestive discomfort.

## Types of Fiber

### Soluble Fiber
Dissolves in water to form a gel-like substance. Soluble fiber slows digestion, helps lower cholesterol, and stabilizes blood sugar. Found in [oats](/oats), beans, [apples](/apple), and citrus fruits.

### Insoluble Fiber
Does not dissolve in water. Insoluble fiber adds bulk to stool and promotes regular bowel movements. Found in whole wheat, vegetables, nuts, and the skins of fruits.

Both types are important, and most high-fiber foods contain a mix of both.

## 30 Best High-Fiber Foods

### Legumes and Beans

**1. [Lentils](/lentils) (cooked) — 7.9g fiber per 100g**
Lentils are fiber superstars. A 200g serving delivers nearly 16g of fiber — over half the daily recommendation in a single dish. They cook faster than other legumes and work well in soups, stews, and salads.

**2. Black Beans (cooked) — 8.7g fiber per 100g**
Among the highest-fiber legumes. A cup of black beans provides about 15g of fiber along with 15g of protein. Pair with [rice](/rice) for a complete protein meal.

**3. Chickpeas (cooked) — 7.6g fiber per 100g**
Used in [hummus](/hummus), curries, and salads. Roasted chickpeas make an excellent high-fiber snack with a satisfying crunch.

**4. Split Peas (cooked) — 8.3g fiber per 100g**
Perfect for thick, hearty soups. Split pea soup is one of the most fiber-rich meals you can make.

**5. Kidney Beans (cooked) — 6.4g fiber per 100g**
A staple in chili, bean salads, and Mexican dishes. Kidney beans provide both soluble and insoluble fiber.

### Whole Grains

**6. [Oats](/oats) (dry) — 10.6g fiber per 100g**
Oats are particularly rich in beta-glucan, a soluble fiber that has been shown to lower cholesterol. A 50g serving of oats provides about 5g of fiber.

**7. Barley (cooked) — 3.8g fiber per 100g**
Another excellent source of beta-glucan. Add barley to soups and stews for a hearty, high-fiber boost.

**8. [Quinoa](/quinoa) (cooked) — 2.8g fiber per 100g**
A gluten-free whole grain that provides fiber along with complete protein. Works as a base for bowls, salads, and side dishes.

**9. Brown [Rice](/rice) (cooked) — 1.8g fiber per 100g**
Contains about three times more fiber than white rice. Choosing brown rice over white is one of the simplest fiber upgrades.

**10. Whole Wheat Bread — 6.8g fiber per 100g**
Two slices of whole wheat bread provide roughly 4g of fiber. Always check labels — look for "100% whole wheat" as the first ingredient.

### Fruits

**11. [Avocado](/avocado) — 6.7g fiber per 100g**
Avocado is technically a fruit and is one of the highest-fiber ones available. Half an avocado provides about 5g of fiber along with healthy monounsaturated fats.

**12. Raspberries — 6.5g fiber per 100g**
One of the highest-fiber fruits. A cup of raspberries delivers about 8g of fiber for just 64 calories.

**13. Pear — 3.1g fiber per 100g**
A medium pear contains about 5.5g of fiber, especially when eaten with the skin. Pears are also gentle on the stomach.

**14. [Apple](/apple) — 2.4g fiber per 100g**
One medium apple with skin provides about 4.4g of fiber. The pectin in apples is a soluble fiber that supports gut health.

**15. [Banana](/banana) — 2.6g fiber per 100g**
A convenient source of fiber, especially green (unripe) bananas which are higher in resistant starch, a type of fiber that feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

**16. [Strawberries](/strawberries) — 2.0g fiber per 100g**
Lower in fiber than raspberries but still a worthwhile source, especially given their low calorie count (32 calories per 100g).

### Vegetables

**17. Artichoke (cooked) — 5.4g fiber per 100g**
One of the highest-fiber vegetables. A single medium artichoke provides about 7g of fiber.

**18. [Broccoli](/broccoli) — 2.6g fiber per 100g**
A versatile vegetable that adds fiber to virtually any meal. Steamed, roasted, or raw, broccoli is a daily fiber contributor.

**19. Brussels Sprouts — 3.8g fiber per 100g**
Roasted Brussels sprouts are not only delicious but deliver meaningful fiber along with vitamin C and vitamin K.

**20. [Sweet Potato](/sweet-potato) — 3.0g fiber per 100g**
A fiber-rich carbohydrate that works as a side dish, in bowls, or mashed. Eating the skin adds extra fiber.

**21. [Carrots](/carrots) — 2.8g fiber per 100g**
Raw carrots make a convenient high-fiber snack. Pair with [hummus](/hummus) for a double fiber boost.

**22. [Spinach](/spinach) (cooked) — 2.4g fiber per 100g**
Cooking spinach concentrates its fiber and makes it easier to eat in larger quantities. A 150g serving of cooked spinach provides about 3.6g of fiber.

**23. Cauliflower — 2.0g fiber per 100g**
Use cauliflower rice as a low-calorie, moderate-fiber alternative to regular [rice](/rice).

### Nuts and Seeds

**24. Chia Seeds — 34.4g fiber per 100g**
Ounce for ounce, chia seeds are the most fiber-dense food on this list. Just 2 tablespoons (20g) deliver nearly 7g of fiber. Add to smoothies, yogurt, or make chia pudding.

**25. Flax Seeds (ground) — 27.3g fiber per 100g**
Another fiber powerhouse. Two tablespoons of ground flax provide about 4g of fiber plus omega-3 fatty acids. Sprinkle on [oats](/oats), yogurt, or salads.

**26. [Almonds](/almonds) — 12.5g fiber per 100g**
A 30g handful of almonds provides about 4g of fiber along with protein and healthy fats.

**27. Pistachios — 10.6g fiber per 100g**
Eating pistachios in their shells naturally slows you down, which helps with portion control. A 30g serving delivers 3g of fiber.

**28. Sunflower Seeds — 8.6g fiber per 100g**
A snack-friendly seed that adds fiber and crunch to salads and trail mixes.

**29. Pumpkin Seeds — 6.5g fiber per 100g**
Rich in fiber, magnesium, and zinc. Roasted pumpkin seeds make an excellent portable snack.

**30. Coconut (dried, unsweetened) — 16.3g fiber per 100g**
Shredded coconut adds fiber to smoothie bowls, energy balls, and baked goods. Use in moderation due to its calorie density.

## How to Increase Fiber Intake Without Discomfort

Adding too much fiber too quickly can cause bloating, gas, and cramping. Follow these tips for a smooth transition:

1. **Increase gradually.** Add 3-5g of additional fiber per week until you reach your target.
2. **Drink more water.** Fiber absorbs water, so increasing fiber without increasing fluid intake can worsen constipation.
3. **Spread fiber across the day.** Eating 30g of fiber in one sitting is far harder to digest than spreading it across 4-5 meals.
4. **Cook your vegetables.** Cooked vegetables are generally easier to digest than raw ones when you are first increasing fiber.
5. **Soak legumes overnight.** This reduces compounds that cause gas and makes beans easier to digest.

## Key Takeaways

- Most adults need 25-30g of fiber per day but only eat about half that amount
- The highest-fiber foods include chia seeds (34g/100g), lentils (8g/100g), black beans (9g/100g), and oats (11g/100g)
- Both soluble and insoluble fiber are important — eat a variety of sources
- Increase fiber gradually (3-5g per week) and drink plenty of water to avoid digestive issues
- Simple swaps like choosing brown [rice](/rice) over white, adding chia seeds to yogurt, and snacking on [almonds](/almonds) can significantly boost daily fiber
