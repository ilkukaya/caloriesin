## Introduction

Eating fewer calories does not have to mean feeling hungry all the time. The secret is choosing foods that are naturally low in calories but high in fiber, water content, or protein — the three nutrients most closely linked to satiety.

The foods on this list all deliver plenty of volume and nutrition for very few calories. Adding more of them to your meals can help you stay within your calorie budget while still feeling satisfied after every meal.

## The 20 Best Low-Calorie Foods

### 1. Broccoli — 34 calories per 100g

[Broccoli](/broccoli) is packed with fiber, vitamin C, and vitamin K. A large serving of steamed broccoli adds significant bulk to any meal for minimal calories. It is also versatile enough to stir-fry, roast, or add to soups.

### 2. Spinach — 23 calories per 100g

[Spinach](/spinach) is one of the most nutrient-dense foods you can eat. A generous handful of raw spinach in a salad or smoothie adds almost no calories while providing iron, folate, and vitamins A and C.

### 3. Watermelon — 30 calories per 100g

[Watermelon](/watermelon) is over 90% water, making it incredibly hydrating and filling for its calorie count. A 300g slice contains just 90 calories and satisfies sweet cravings naturally.

### 4. Cucumber — 15 calories per 100g

Cucumbers are one of the lowest-calorie foods available. They make an excellent snack on their own or sliced into salads and sandwiches for added crunch without adding meaningful calories.

### 5. Strawberries — 32 calories per 100g

Strawberries offer natural sweetness along with fiber and vitamin C. A full cup of sliced strawberries has only about 53 calories, making them one of the best dessert swaps available.

### 6. Celery — 16 calories per 100g

Celery is mostly water and fiber. While the myth that it takes more calories to digest than it contains is not quite true, it is still an excellent low-calorie snack that provides satisfying crunch.

### 7. Cauliflower — 25 calories per 100g

Cauliflower has become a popular substitute for higher-calorie foods like rice, pizza crust, and mashed potatoes. A full cup of cauliflower rice has only about 25 calories compared to 200 or more for regular rice.

### 8. Tomatoes — 18 calories per 100g

Tomatoes are rich in lycopene, vitamin C, and potassium. They add flavor and substance to salads, sandwiches, and cooked dishes with virtually no calorie cost.

### 9. Zucchini — 17 calories per 100g

Zucchini works as a pasta substitute when spiralized into noodles. A full plate of zucchini noodles has roughly 30 calories compared to 200 or more for a standard serving of pasta.

### 10. Mushrooms — 22 calories per 100g

Mushrooms add a savory, meaty flavor to dishes for very few calories. They also contain B vitamins and selenium. Sauteed mushrooms make a great low-calorie addition to omelets, salads, and stir-fries.

### 11. Bell Peppers — 31 calories per 100g

Bell peppers are sweet, crunchy, and loaded with vitamin C. One whole bell pepper has only about 45 calories and works well as a snack with hummus or as a base for stuffed pepper recipes.

### 12. Lettuce (Romaine) — 17 calories per 100g

Romaine lettuce provides a crisp base for salads and wraps. Using lettuce wraps instead of tortillas or bread can save 100 to 200 calories per meal.

### 13. Carrots — 41 calories per 100g

Carrots are slightly higher in calories than other vegetables on this list but are rich in beta-carotene and very filling due to their crunch and fiber content. A medium carrot has about 25 calories.

### 14. Egg Whites — 52 calories per 100g

Egg whites are almost pure protein with no fat. Three egg whites contain about 51 calories and 11 grams of protein, making them an excellent way to increase protein intake without adding excess calories.

### 15. Greek Yogurt (Non-Fat) — 59 calories per 100g

Non-fat Greek yogurt delivers roughly 10 grams of protein per 100g for under 60 calories. The protein and thick texture make it one of the most satisfying low-calorie snack options.

### 16. Cabbage — 25 calories per 100g

Cabbage is a fiber-rich vegetable that works well in slaws, stir-fries, and soups. It is inexpensive, stores well in the fridge, and adds plenty of volume to meals.

### 17. Asparagus — 20 calories per 100g

Asparagus is a good source of folate and vitamins A, C, and K. Six spears of asparagus contain only about 20 calories, making it an ideal side dish for any protein.

### 18. Grapefruit — 42 calories per 100g

Half a grapefruit has about 52 calories and is high in fiber and water. Some research suggests that eating grapefruit before meals may help reduce overall calorie intake.

### 19. Cod — 82 calories per 100g

While not as low as vegetables, cod is one of the leanest protein sources available. A 150g fillet has about 123 calories and 26 grams of protein, keeping you full for hours.

### 20. Air-Popped Popcorn — 31 calories per cup

Without butter or oil, air-popped popcorn is a surprisingly low-calorie whole grain snack. Three cups contain under 100 calories and provide a satisfying crunch that lasts much longer than a handful of chips.

## Tips for Building Low-Calorie Meals

- **Fill half your plate with vegetables.** This simple habit dramatically reduces the calorie density of any meal.
- **Start meals with a broth-based soup or salad.** Research shows that eating a low-calorie starter reduces total calorie intake for the meal.
- **Add protein to every meal.** Protein is the most satiating macronutrient and helps prevent overeating later.
- **Drink water before and during meals.** Thirst is sometimes mistaken for hunger, and water helps fill the stomach.
- **Season generously.** Herbs, spices, lemon juice, and vinegar add bold flavor to low-calorie foods without adding meaningful calories.

Building your meals around these low-calorie, nutrient-dense foods is one of the most effective strategies for sustainable weight management. You can eat large, satisfying portions while staying within your calorie goals.
