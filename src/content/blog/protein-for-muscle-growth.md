## Introduction

Protein is the raw material your body uses to build and repair muscle tissue. Without adequate protein, your muscles cannot recover from training or grow larger and stronger, no matter how hard you work out. But how much protein do you actually need? The answer depends on your body weight, training intensity, and goals.

Decades of research have narrowed down the optimal protein range for muscle growth. This guide covers the science, practical recommendations, and the best food sources to hit your daily target.

## The Science: How Much Protein for Muscle Growth?

### The Research Consensus

A landmark 2018 meta-analysis published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine analyzed 49 studies and over 1,800 participants. The findings:

- **1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day** is the point at which muscle growth is maximized for most people doing resistance training
- Benefits plateau around **2.2g per kg** — eating more than this does not produce additional muscle growth
- **The optimal range is 1.6-2.2g per kg per day**

### What This Means in Practice

| Body Weight | Minimum (1.6g/kg) | Optimal (2.0g/kg) | Upper Limit (2.2g/kg) |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| 60 kg (132 lb) | 96g/day | 120g/day | 132g/day |
| 70 kg (154 lb) | 112g/day | 140g/day | 154g/day |
| 80 kg (176 lb) | 128g/day | 160g/day | 176g/day |
| 90 kg (198 lb) | 144g/day | 180g/day | 198g/day |
| 100 kg (220 lb) | 160g/day | 200g/day | 220g/day |

For most people, aiming for **2.0g per kg** provides a comfortable margin above the minimum effective dose.

### Do You Need More During a Cut?

Yes. When you are in a calorie deficit (cutting fat), your protein needs increase. A 2011 study found that athletes in a calorie deficit who consumed 2.3g/kg retained significantly more lean mass than those consuming 1.0g/kg.

During a cut, aim for the higher end of the range: **1.8-2.4g per kg per day**.

## Protein Timing: Does It Matter?

### The Anabolic Window

The idea that you must consume protein within 30 minutes of your workout or "lose your gains" is largely a myth. While post-workout protein is beneficial, the window is much wider than 30 minutes.

Research shows that as long as you consume protein **within 2-3 hours** before or after your workout, muscle protein synthesis is effectively stimulated.

### Protein Distribution Matters More

What does matter is how you distribute protein across the day. Research consistently shows that spreading protein intake across 3-5 meals is superior to loading most of your protein into one meal.

**Why:** Your body can only stimulate maximum muscle protein synthesis with about 0.4-0.55g per kg per meal (roughly 30-40g for most people). Eating 100g of protein in one meal does not build more muscle than eating 40g — the excess is used for energy or other bodily functions.

**Optimal distribution for an 80kg person (160g daily):**

| Meal | Timing | Protein |
|------|--------|---------|
| Breakfast | 7:00 AM | 35g |
| Lunch | 12:00 PM | 40g |
| Post-Workout Snack | 4:00 PM | 30g |
| Dinner | 7:00 PM | 40g |
| Evening Snack | 9:00 PM | 15g |

## Best Protein Sources for Muscle Growth

### Tier 1: Complete, High-Quality Animal Proteins

These foods provide all essential amino acids in high concentrations and are the most efficient for muscle building.

**[Chicken Breast](/chicken-breast) — 31g protein per 100g (165 cal)**
The gold standard for lean muscle-building protein. A 200g breast delivers 62g of protein for 330 calories.

**[Eggs](/eggs) — 6g protein per egg (72 cal)**
Complete protein with excellent bioavailability. Three eggs at breakfast provides 18g of protein and all essential amino acids.

**[Salmon](/salmon) — 20g protein per 100g (208 cal)**
Provides protein plus omega-3 fatty acids, which may reduce exercise-induced inflammation and support recovery.

**Lean [Beef](/beef) — 26g protein per 100g (150-250 cal)**
Rich in protein, creatine, iron, and zinc — all important for muscle growth and performance. Choose sirloin, tenderloin, or 95% lean ground beef.

**[Turkey Breast](/turkey-breast) — 29g protein per 100g (135 cal)**
Nearly as lean as chicken breast with an excellent protein-to-calorie ratio.

**Greek Yogurt — 10g protein per 100g (59 cal)**
Contains both whey and casein protein, making it useful anytime. A 200g serving provides 20g of protein.

**[Tuna](/tuna) — 26g protein per 100g (116 cal)**
One of the most protein-dense foods per calorie. Convenient canned tuna makes hitting protein targets easy.

### Tier 2: Plant-Based Proteins

**Tempeh — 19g protein per 100g (192 cal)**
The highest-protein whole food plant option. Fermented soybeans with a firm texture.

**[Tofu](/tofu) (firm) — 8g protein per 100g (76 cal)**
Versatile and mild-flavored. You need larger portions than animal proteins to match the protein content.

**[Lentils](/lentils) — 9g protein per 100g cooked (116 cal)**
Combine with grains like [rice](/rice) for a complete amino acid profile.

**[Quinoa](/quinoa) — 4g protein per 100g cooked (120 cal)**
One of the few plant foods that is a complete protein, though the protein amount per serving is modest.

**Edamame — 11g protein per 100g (121 cal)**
High in protein and a complete protein source. A 200g serving delivers 22g of protein.

### Tier 3: Supplements (When Needed)

**Whey Protein — 24-30g protein per scoop (100-130 cal)**
The most researched protein supplement. Whey digests quickly and contains high leucine, the amino acid most important for triggering muscle protein synthesis.

**Casein Protein — 24-28g protein per scoop (110-130 cal)**
Digests slowly over 6-8 hours. Best before bed to provide amino acids during sleep.

## Common Protein Myths

### "Too much protein damages your kidneys"
In healthy individuals, high protein intake does not harm kidney function. This concern applies only to people with pre-existing kidney disease. Multiple large-scale studies have found no kidney damage in healthy adults consuming up to 3g/kg/day.

### "You can only absorb 30g of protein per meal"
Your body can absorb more than 30g of protein per meal — it just may not use all of it for muscle protein synthesis. The excess is used for other bodily functions or converted to energy. However, distributing protein across meals does optimize muscle building.

### "Plant protein cannot build muscle"
Plant protein can absolutely build muscle, though you may need slightly more total protein (about 10-20% more) because plant proteins tend to have lower leucine content and digestibility.

## Key Takeaways

- The optimal protein intake for muscle growth is 1.6-2.2g per kg of body weight per day
- During a calorie deficit, aim for the higher end (1.8-2.4g/kg) to preserve muscle
- Distribute protein across 3-5 meals with 30-40g per meal for optimal muscle protein synthesis
- The "anabolic window" is not 30 minutes — consuming protein within 2-3 hours of training is sufficient
- [Chicken breast](/chicken-breast), [eggs](/eggs), [salmon](/salmon), Greek yogurt, and lean [beef](/beef) are the most effective whole food protein sources
- Protein supplements are convenient but not superior to whole food sources
