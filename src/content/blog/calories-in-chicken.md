## Introduction

Chicken is the world's most popular lean protein source and a cornerstone of nearly every diet plan, from bodybuilding to weight loss. But not all chicken is created equal — the calorie and fat content varies dramatically between cuts and cooking methods. A skinless [chicken breast](/chicken-breast) and a fried chicken wing are entirely different foods from a nutritional standpoint.

This guide breaks down the calories in every common cut of chicken, compares skin-on vs. skinless, and shows how cooking methods affect the final calorie count.

| Cut | Calories per 100g | Protein per 100g | Fat per 100g |
|-----|-------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| [Chicken Breast](/chicken-breast) | 165 cal | 31g | 3.6g |
| Chicken Tenderloin | 161 cal | 30g | 3.2g |
| Chicken Thigh (skinless) | 209 cal | 26g | 10.9g |
| Chicken Drumstick (skinless) | 172 cal | 28g | 5.7g |
| Chicken Wing (skinless) | 203 cal | 30g | 8.1g |
| Chicken Leg (thigh + drum, skinless) | 191 cal | 27g | 8.4g |
| Ground Chicken (lean) | 150 cal | 21g | 7.0g |
| Ground Chicken (regular) | 189 cal | 20g | 11g |

[Chicken breast](/chicken-breast) is the clear winner for calorie-conscious eaters. At 165 calories and 31g of protein per 100g, it has the highest protein-to-calorie ratio of any chicken cut. A 150g breast provides 46g of protein for just 248 calories.

## Skin-On vs. Skinless

Chicken skin is mostly fat and significantly increases the calorie count of any cut:

| Cut | Skinless (per 100g) | Skin-On (per 100g) | Extra Calories |
|-----|---------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Breast | 165 cal | 197 cal | +32 cal |
| Thigh | 209 cal | 229 cal | +20 cal |
| Drumstick | 172 cal | 216 cal | +44 cal |
| Wing | 203 cal | 290 cal | +87 cal |

Wings see the biggest impact because skin makes up a large proportion of the wing's total weight. A single skin-on chicken wing has about 99 calories, compared to 43 calories for the small amount of meat without skin.

**The verdict:** If you are watching calories, always remove the skin. If you are at maintenance or bulking, skin-on chicken is fine and adds flavor.

## Calories by Cooking Method

How you cook chicken matters as much as which cut you choose.

### Grilled Chicken Breast — 165 cal per 100g
Grilling adds no extra calories (assuming no oil) and is one of the best methods for lean chicken. The high heat creates flavor through browning.

### Baked/Roasted Chicken Breast — 165 cal per 100g
Baking with minimal oil is equally low-calorie. Season with herbs and spices for flavor without extra calories.

### Pan-Seared Chicken Breast — 185-210 cal per 100g
Pan-searing typically uses 1-2 teaspoons of oil, adding 40-80 calories to the total. Using cooking spray instead brings this close to the grilled calorie count.

### Fried Chicken (Breaded) — 260-300 cal per 100g
Breading and deep-frying nearly doubles the calorie count of chicken breast. The flour coating absorbs oil, adding both fat and carbohydrate calories.

### Rotisserie Chicken — 190-230 cal per 100g
Store-bought rotisserie chicken falls between grilled and fried. The skin adds calories, and many stores season with oils and sugars. Removing the skin drops it to roughly 165-180 cal per 100g.

### Chicken Nuggets — 280-320 cal per 100g
Heavily processed and breaded, chicken nuggets contain more breading and oil than actual chicken in many cases.

## Dark Meat vs. White Meat

### White Meat (Breast, Tenderloin)
- Lower in calories and fat
- Higher in protein per calorie
- Leaner, milder flavor
- Can dry out if overcooked
- **Best for:** Weight loss, high-protein diets

### Dark Meat (Thigh, Drumstick, Wing)
- Higher in calories and fat
- More flavorful and juicy
- More forgiving when cooking (harder to dry out)
- Contains more iron and zinc than white meat
- **Best for:** Flavor, slow cooking, stews, meal prep

Both white and dark meat are excellent protein sources. If calories are your primary concern, white meat is the better choice. If flavor and cooking flexibility matter more, dark meat is perfectly healthy.

## Tips for Keeping Chicken Calories Low

1. **Choose breast or tenderloin** for the leanest option
2. **Remove the skin** before or after cooking to cut 20-87 calories per 100g
3. **Grill, bake, or poach** instead of frying
4. **Use cooking spray** instead of oil when pan-cooking
5. **Watch your sauces** — BBQ sauce, teriyaki, and cream-based sauces can add 50-200+ calories per serving
6. **Season with herbs and spices** — garlic, paprika, cumin, rosemary, and lemon add flavor for zero calories
7. **Weigh your portions** — chicken breasts vary wildly in size (120g to 300g+)

## Meal Prep With Chicken

Chicken breast is the ultimate meal prep protein. Here is a simple approach:

1. Buy 1-1.5 kg of [chicken breast](/chicken-breast) on Sunday
2. Season with different spice blends (Italian, Mexican, Asian, lemon herb)
3. Bake at 200C/400F for 20-25 minutes until internal temperature reaches 74C/165F
4. Slice and store in separate containers
5. Pair throughout the week with different sides: [rice](/rice), [quinoa](/quinoa), [sweet potato](/sweet-potato), and steamed vegetables like [broccoli](/broccoli) or [asparagus](/asparagus)

This provides 5+ days of ready-to-eat protein at about 165 calories per 100g.

## Key Takeaways

- [Chicken breast](/chicken-breast) is the leanest cut at 165 cal and 31g protein per 100g
- Thighs have more fat (209 cal/100g skinless) but more flavor
- Skin adds 20-87 extra calories per 100g depending on the cut
- Grilling and baking keep calories lowest; frying nearly doubles them
- A single grilled breast (150g) provides 248 calories and 46g of protein
- Chicken is the ideal meal prep protein — cook once, eat all week
