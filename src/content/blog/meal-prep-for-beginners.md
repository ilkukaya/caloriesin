## Introduction

Meal prep is the practice of planning, preparing, and portioning meals in advance — typically on a single day each week. It is the single most effective strategy for eating consistently well, saving money, reducing food waste, and staying on track with nutrition goals.

If the idea of cooking an entire week of food in one afternoon sounds overwhelming, relax. Meal prep does not have to mean spending six hours in the kitchen. This guide starts simple and scales up gradually over four weeks, so you build the habit without burning out.

## Why Meal Prep Works

The reason most diets fail is not a lack of knowledge — it is a lack of preparation. When you come home tired and hungry with no plan, you order takeout. When you open the fridge and find prepped containers of [chicken breast](/chicken-breast), [rice](/rice), and roasted vegetables, you eat a balanced meal in minutes.

**The evidence:**
- People who meal prep eat more fruits and vegetables and less fast food
- Meal preppers have more food variety than those who cook on the fly
- Consistent meal preppers spend 30-50% less on food than those who eat out regularly
- Having pre-portioned meals eliminates the decision fatigue that leads to poor food choices

## What You Need to Get Started

### Equipment
- **Meal prep containers** (12-15 compartmented containers, glass or BPA-free plastic)
- **A kitchen food scale** for accurate portioning
- **Two baking sheets** for roasting
- **A large pot** for grains and legumes
- **A sharp knife and cutting board**
- **Aluminum foil or parchment paper** for easy cleanup

### Pantry Staples
- [Rice](/rice) (white or brown)
- [Oats](/oats)
- [Quinoa](/quinoa)
- Olive oil and cooking spray
- Salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika, cumin, Italian seasoning
- Soy sauce
- Canned [tuna](/tuna) and canned beans

## Week 1: Start Simple (2 Hours)

Your first week, keep it manageable. Prep just two components: a protein and a grain.

### Sunday Prep List

**Protein: Baked Chicken Breast (1 kg)**
1. Season 1 kg of [chicken breast](/chicken-breast) with olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder, and paprika
2. Bake at 200C/400F for 22-25 minutes until internal temperature reaches 74C/165F
3. Let rest 5 minutes, then slice
4. Divide into 5 containers (~200g each, 330 calories, 62g protein per container)

**Grain: Brown Rice (500g dry)**
1. Cook 500g dry brown [rice](/rice) according to package directions
2. This yields about 1.5 kg of cooked rice
3. Divide into 5 containers (~300g each, 370 calories per container)

**Total prep time:** About 40 minutes active, 25 minutes passive (while things cook).

**Each day:** Combine chicken + rice with whatever fresh vegetables you have on hand. Steam [broccoli](/broccoli), add [spinach](/spinach), or toss in a salad. Daily cost: about $3-4.

## Week 2: Add Vegetables and Variety (2.5 Hours)

Now add roasted vegetables and a second protein option.

### Sunday Prep List

**Protein 1: Seasoned Chicken Breast (750g)**
Same method as Week 1, but with Italian seasoning.

**Protein 2: Hard-Boiled Eggs (12 [eggs](/eggs))**
1. Place 12 eggs in a pot, cover with cold water
2. Bring to a boil, then cover and remove from heat
3. Let sit 12 minutes, then transfer to ice water
4. Peel and store. Each egg: 78 calories, 6g protein.

**Grain: Quinoa (400g dry)**
1. Cook [quinoa](/quinoa) with a pinch of salt
2. Divide into 5 portions of ~240g cooked (288 cal each)

**Vegetables: Sheet Pan Roasted Mix**
1. Cut 2 [sweet potatoes](/sweet-potato), 2 [zucchinis](/zucchini), 1 head of [broccoli](/broccoli), and 2 [bell peppers](/bell-peppers) into bite-sized pieces
2. Toss with 1 tablespoon olive oil, salt, pepper, and garlic powder
3. Spread across two baking sheets
4. Roast at 200C/400F for 25-30 minutes
5. Divide into 5 portions

## Week 3: Full Meal Assembly (3 Hours)

By now you are comfortable with the basics. This week, assemble complete meals in containers.

### Meal 1: Chicken, Rice, and Broccoli (x3)
- 180g [chicken breast](/chicken-breast), sliced — 297 cal
- 150g cooked [rice](/rice) — 195 cal
- 120g steamed [broccoli](/broccoli) — 41 cal
- **Per container: 533 calories, 58g protein**

### Meal 2: Salmon with Sweet Potato and Asparagus (x2)
- 150g [salmon](/salmon), baked — 312 cal
- 130g [sweet potato](/sweet-potato), cubed and roasted — 112 cal
- 100g [asparagus](/asparagus), roasted — 20 cal
- **Per container: 444 calories, 33g protein**

### Breakfast Prep: Overnight Oats (x5)
In 5 jars or containers:
- 40g [oats](/oats) — 152 cal
- 150ml milk — 75 cal
- 10g chia seeds — 49 cal
- 80g [blueberries](/blueberries) (add morning-of) — 46 cal
- **Per jar: ~322 calories, 13g protein**

### Snack Prep
- 10 hard-boiled [eggs](/eggs) — grab 2 per day (156 cal, 12g protein)
- Cut [carrots](/carrots), [cucumber](/cucumber), and [bell peppers](/bell-peppers) into sticks — store in water in the fridge
- 5 portions of [almonds](/almonds) (20g each) in small bags — 116 cal each

## Week 4: Diversify and Optimize (3-4 Hours)

By week 4, you have a system. Now add variety to prevent boredom.

### Batch Cook Two Different Proteins

**Protein A: Herb-Crusted Chicken Thighs**
Season with rosemary, thyme, and lemon. Bake at 200C for 30 minutes. Thighs (skinless) provide 209 calories and 26g protein per 100g.

**Protein B: Turkey Taco Meat**
Brown 500g lean ground [turkey](/turkey-breast) with cumin, chili powder, garlic, and onion. Divide into 4 portions. Each portion (~125g): 200 cal, 25g protein.

### Make Two Different Grain Bases

**Grain A: Cilantro Lime Rice** — Cook white [rice](/rice) and stir in lime juice and chopped cilantro.

**Grain B: Lemon Herb Quinoa** — Cook [quinoa](/quinoa) and toss with lemon zest, parsley, and a drizzle of olive oil.

### Prep Sauces and Dressings
Make 2-3 sauces to rotate throughout the week:
- Lemon tahini dressing (mix tahini, lemon juice, water, garlic)
- Simple teriyaki (soy sauce, honey, garlic, ginger)
- Greek yogurt ranch (yogurt, dill, garlic powder, lemon)

Sauces prevent meal prep boredom more than anything else. The same chicken and rice bowl tastes completely different with teriyaki versus tahini dressing.

## Meal Prep Storage Guide

| Food | Fridge Life | Freezer Life |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Cooked chicken | 4 days | 3 months |
| Cooked rice/quinoa | 5 days | 3 months |
| Roasted vegetables | 4 days | 2 months |
| Hard-boiled eggs | 7 days | Not recommended |
| Overnight oats | 5 days | Not recommended |
| Cooked ground meat | 4 days | 3 months |
| Cooked salmon/fish | 3 days | 2 months |

**Tip:** Prep meals for Monday-Wednesday and store in the fridge. Freeze Thursday-Friday meals and thaw them in the fridge overnight on Wednesday.

## Common Meal Prep Mistakes

1. **Prepping too many recipes at once.** Start with 2-3 meals, not 7 different recipes.
2. **Not seasoning enough.** Bland meal prep is the number one reason people quit. Use plenty of spices, herbs, and sauces.
3. **Ignoring food safety.** Cool cooked food within 2 hours and refrigerate promptly. Do not leave containers at room temperature.
4. **Eating the same thing every day.** Use different sauces and seasonings to create variety from the same base ingredients.
5. **Skipping the food scale.** Without weighing portions, your calorie counts are guesses.
6. **Overcooking chicken breast.** Use a meat thermometer and pull it at 74C/165F. Let it rest before slicing.

## Key Takeaways

- Meal prep saves time, money, and dramatically improves diet consistency
- Start simple in week 1 (just a protein and a grain) and build complexity gradually
- Batch cook 2 proteins, 1-2 grains, and a tray of roasted vegetables as your foundation
- Use different sauces and seasonings to create variety from the same base ingredients
- Store fridge meals for 3-5 days and freeze anything beyond that
- A kitchen food scale is essential for accurate portions and calorie counting
- Meal prep does not have to take all day — 2-3 hours on Sunday covers the entire week
