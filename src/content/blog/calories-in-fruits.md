## Introduction

Fruits are nature's fast food — portable, naturally sweet, and packed with vitamins, minerals, and fiber. But if you are counting calories or managing your weight, you might wonder how different fruits stack up. The calorie content of fruits varies considerably, from just 15 calories per 100g for starfruit to over 200 calories per 100g for dried dates.

This guide breaks down the calorie content of over 40 common fruits, organized from lowest to highest calorie per 100g. Use it as a quick reference whenever you need to check a fruit's calorie count.

[Watermelon](/watermelon) stands out as particularly diet-friendly. A generous 300g wedge has just 90 calories and its high water content (92%) makes it extremely filling relative to its calorie count.

## Fresh vs. Dried vs. Frozen Fruit Calories

### Fresh Fruit
The calorie counts above reflect fresh fruit. This is the best option for weight management because the high water content keeps calories low and satiety high.

### Dried Fruit
Drying removes water, concentrating calories dramatically. 100g of fresh grapes has 67 calories, but 100g of raisins has 299 calories. Dried fruits are nutrient-dense but very easy to overeat.

### Frozen Fruit
Frozen fruit has essentially the same calorie content as fresh fruit. It is frozen at peak ripeness and retains its nutritional value. Frozen berries, [mango](/mango), and [banana](/banana) are excellent for smoothies and are often more affordable than fresh.

### Fruit Juice
Juicing removes fiber and makes it easy to consume large amounts of sugar quickly. A cup of orange juice (112 calories) has more calories than an actual orange (62 calories) and is far less filling.

## How Much Fruit Should You Eat?

Most nutrition guidelines recommend 2-4 servings of fruit per day. A serving is typically:

- 1 medium fruit ([apple](/apple), [banana](/banana), orange)
- 1 cup of cut fruit
- 1/2 cup of dried fruit
- 3/4 cup of 100% fruit juice

For weight loss, prioritize whole fresh fruits over juice or dried fruit. The fiber and water content of whole fruit promotes fullness and slows sugar absorption.

## Key Takeaways

- Fruit calories range from 15 cal/100g (starfruit) to 354 cal/100g (coconut)
- The lowest-calorie fruits are melons and berries — eat these freely
- [Banana](/banana) (89 cal/100g) and grapes (67 cal/100g) are on the higher end but still healthy choices
- Dried fruits are calorie-dense because water has been removed — watch portions
- Whole fresh fruit is always preferable to juice for weight management
- Most adults should eat 2-4 servings of fruit daily, prioritizing variety
