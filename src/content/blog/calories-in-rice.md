## Introduction

[Rice](/rice) is a dietary staple for more than half the world's population. It is affordable, versatile, and pairs with virtually any protein or vegetable. But rice is also one of the most common foods people miscalculate when counting calories, largely because the difference between dry and cooked weights can be confusing.

This guide covers the calorie content of every common type of rice, explains the dry vs. cooked distinction, and helps you understand how rice fits into different diet plans.

## Rice Calories: Cooked vs. Dry

The most important thing to understand about rice calories is the difference between dry and cooked measurements. Rice roughly triples in weight when cooked because it absorbs water.

**100g of dry white rice = approximately 300g of cooked white rice**

This means:
- 100g dry white rice = ~360 calories
- 100g cooked white rice = ~130 calories

Always check whether a calorie source refers to dry or cooked rice. This single distinction is responsible for more calorie tracking errors than almost any other food.

## Detailed Breakdown by Type

### White Rice
The most consumed type of rice globally. White rice has had its bran and germ removed, which strips away most of the fiber and some nutrients but gives it a lighter texture and longer shelf life. At 130 calories per 100g cooked, it is a moderate-calorie carb source.

**Best for:** Stir-fries, curries, grain bowls, and sushi. Pairs well with [chicken breast](/chicken-breast), [salmon](/salmon), or [tofu](/tofu).

### Brown Rice
Brown rice retains its bran layer, providing more fiber (1.8g vs. 0.4g per 100g), more minerals, and a nuttier flavor. It has slightly fewer calories than white rice and takes longer to digest, which helps with blood sugar control.

**Best for:** Health-conscious meals, bowls with grilled [chicken](/chicken-breast) and vegetables, or as a fiber-rich side dish.

### Basmati Rice
Popular in Indian and Middle Eastern cuisines, basmati has a distinctive aromatic quality and a lower glycemic index than other white rice varieties. It has slightly fewer calories (121 cal per 100g cooked) and slightly more protein.

**Best for:** Curries, biryanis, and as a fragrant side dish with grilled meats and vegetables.

### Jasmine Rice
A fragrant, slightly sticky long-grain rice popular in Thai and Southeast Asian cooking. Calorie-wise it is similar to standard white rice (129 cal/100g cooked).

**Best for:** Thai curries, stir-fries, and dishes where a slightly sticky texture is desirable.

### Wild Rice
Technically a grass seed rather than true rice, wild rice is the lowest-calorie option at 101 calories per 100g cooked. It also has the highest protein content (4g per 100g) and the most fiber among rice varieties.

**Best for:** Soups, pilafs, stuffings, and salads. Its chewy texture and nutty flavor make it a great addition to grain mixes.

### Black Rice (Forbidden Rice)
Named for its striking deep purple-black color, black rice is rich in anthocyanin antioxidants (the same compounds found in blueberries). At 115 calories per 100g cooked, it is lower-calorie than white rice with more fiber and protein.

**Best for:** Grain bowls, salads, and as a visually striking side dish.

## Common Rice Portions and Calories

Here is what typical rice portions look like in practice:

| Portion | White Rice | Brown Rice | Basmati |
|---------|-----------|------------|---------|
| Small (75g cooked) | 98 cal | 92 cal | 91 cal |
| Medium (150g cooked) | 195 cal | 185 cal | 182 cal |
| Large (200g cooked) | 260 cal | 246 cal | 242 cal |
| Restaurant serving (~300g) | 390 cal | 369 cal | 363 cal |

A typical restaurant serving of rice is often 250-350g cooked, which can contain 325-455 calories. When eating out, consider asking for a half portion of rice and filling the rest of your plate with vegetables.

## Rice in Popular Dishes

The calorie count of rice-based dishes increases significantly when other ingredients are added:

- **Plain steamed rice** (150g): 195 calories
- **Fried rice** (150g): 250-350 calories (oil and soy sauce add calories)
- **Coconut rice** (150g): 250-300 calories (coconut milk adds fat)
- **Sushi rice** (150g): 220-240 calories (sugar and vinegar are added)
- **Rice pilaf** (150g): 220-260 calories (cooked in broth with butter)
- **Risotto** (200g): 250-350 calories (butter, cheese, wine)
- **Burrito bowl rice** (200g): 260-300 calories (often cooked with oil and lime)

## Lower-Calorie Rice Alternatives

If you want to reduce the calorie impact of your rice portions, consider these alternatives:

| Alternative | Calories per 100g | Calories Saved vs. White Rice |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Cauliflower rice | 25 cal | 105 cal per 100g |
| Shirataki rice | 6 cal | 124 cal per 100g |
| [Quinoa](/quinoa) (cooked) | 120 cal | 10 cal per 100g |
| Bulgur wheat (cooked) | 83 cal | 47 cal per 100g |
| Couscous (cooked) | 112 cal | 18 cal per 100g |

Cauliflower rice is the most dramatic swap, saving over 100 calories per 100g. You can also mix cauliflower rice 50/50 with regular rice to cut calories while maintaining some of the texture and taste you enjoy.

## Tips for Managing Rice Calories

1. **Weigh your portions.** Rice is one of the foods most commonly over-portioned. Use a food scale and weigh cooked rice to know exactly what you are eating.
2. **Choose wild or brown rice** for more fiber and slightly fewer calories.
3. **Let rice cool slightly** before eating. Cooling converts some starch into resistant starch, which your body absorbs fewer calories from.
4. **Pair rice with protein and vegetables.** A bowl of [chicken breast](/chicken-breast) (248 cal for 150g), steamed [broccoli](/broccoli) (51 cal for 150g), and rice (195 cal for 150g) gives you a balanced 494-calorie meal.
5. **Try cauliflower rice** as a swap or mix-in to cut calories without sacrificing volume.

## Key Takeaways

- Cooked white rice has 130 calories per 100g; brown rice has 123 calories per 100g
- Always check whether calorie information refers to dry or cooked rice — dry rice has roughly 3x the calories per weight
- Wild rice is the lowest-calorie, highest-protein variety (101 cal, 4g protein per 100g cooked)
- Typical restaurant rice portions (250-350g) contain 325-455 calories
- Cauliflower rice (25 cal/100g) is the most effective low-calorie substitute
- Weigh your rice portions for accurate calorie tracking — eyeballing is notoriously unreliable for grains
