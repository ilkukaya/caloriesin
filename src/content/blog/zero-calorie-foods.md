## Introduction

The idea of zero calorie foods is seductive: eat as much as you want without gaining weight. But do foods with truly zero calories actually exist? The short answer is that almost no whole food contains literally zero calories, but many foods are so low in calories that their energy contribution is negligible — and a few may come close to "calorie neutral" when you factor in the energy your body uses to digest them.

This guide separates fact from fiction, covers the science behind so-called "negative calorie foods," and provides a comprehensive list of the lowest-calorie foods you can eat freely without worrying about your waistline.

## The Truth About Zero Calorie Foods

Strictly speaking, the only truly zero-calorie items you can consume are water, plain black coffee, and plain tea (unsweetened). Every whole food — even [celery](/celery) and [lettuce](/lettuce) — contains some calories.

However, many foods are so low in calories that they are effectively negligible. A food with 5-15 calories per 100g contributes so little energy that you would struggle to eat enough of it to meaningfully affect your daily calorie total.

## What About Negative Calorie Foods?

The "negative calorie" theory suggests that some foods require more energy to digest than they contain, resulting in a net calorie loss. While this concept is appealing, the science does not fully support it.

The thermic effect of food (TEF) — the energy your body uses to digest, absorb, and metabolize nutrients — accounts for roughly:

- **5-10%** of calories for fats
- **5-10%** of calories for carbohydrates
- **20-30%** of calories for protein

For a food like celery (16 calories per 100g), your body might burn 1-2 calories digesting it. That does not make it "negative calorie," but it does mean the net calorie contribution is extremely small.

**The practical takeaway:** While no food is truly "negative calorie," the foods below are so low in calories that you can eat generous portions of them without meaningfully impacting your calorie budget.

## The Lowest Calorie Foods

### Vegetables (Under 20 Calories per 100g)

**[Celery](/celery) — 16 calories per 100g**
Mostly water and fiber. The quintessential "zero calorie food." Eat it raw with [hummus](/hummus) (though hummus adds calories) or chop it into soups and salads.

**[Cucumber](/cucumber) — 15 calories per 100g**
At 95% water, cucumber is one of the most hydrating foods available. Slice it into salads, use it in place of crackers for dips, or infuse water with it.

**[Lettuce](/lettuce) (iceberg) — 14 calories per 100g**
One of the lowest-calorie foods on the planet. Use lettuce leaves as wraps instead of tortillas to save significant calories.

**[Zucchini](/zucchini) — 17 calories per 100g**
Spiralize it into "zoodles" as a pasta substitute for a meal with a fraction of the calories.

**[Spinach](/spinach) (raw) — 23 calories per 100g**
Slightly higher than the others but incredibly nutrient-dense. A large handful of raw spinach in a salad adds virtually no calories while delivering iron, folate, and vitamins.

**Radishes — 16 calories per 100g**
Peppery and crunchy. Radishes make a refreshing snack or salad addition with negligible calories.

**[Tomatoes](/tomatoes) — 18 calories per 100g**
A medium tomato has about 22 calories. Tomatoes add flavor, color, and lycopene to meals for almost no caloric cost.

**[Mushrooms](/mushrooms) — 22 calories per 100g**
Savory and versatile. Mushrooms add umami depth to dishes while keeping calories minimal.

**[Bell Peppers](/bell-peppers) — 20-31 calories per 100g**
Green peppers are lower in calories (20 cal/100g) while red, yellow, and orange are slightly higher (31 cal/100g) but sweeter.

**Asparagus — 20 calories per 100g**
Roasted or grilled asparagus is a low-calorie side dish that pairs well with virtually any protein.

### Fruits (Under 35 Calories per 100g)

**[Watermelon](/watermelon) — 30 calories per 100g**
Over 90% water with a naturally sweet taste. A 200g slice has just 60 calories and satisfies sweet cravings.

**[Strawberries](/strawberries) — 32 calories per 100g**
A full cup of sliced strawberries contains about 53 calories. They are sweet, fiber-rich, and loaded with vitamin C.

**Cantaloupe — 34 calories per 100g**
Sweet, refreshing, and extremely hydrating. A large wedge of cantaloupe has fewer calories than a single cookie.

**Grapefruit — 33 calories per 100g**
Tart and satisfying, half a grapefruit has about 52 calories. Some research suggests grapefruit may help with insulin sensitivity.

**Papaya — 32 calories per 100g**
Tropical, sweet, and contains papain, an enzyme that aids protein digestion.

### Beverages and Others (0-5 Calories)

**Water — 0 calories**
The only true zero-calorie option. Aim for at least 2 liters per day.

**Black Coffee — 2 calories per cup**
Effectively zero calories. Coffee also slightly boosts metabolism temporarily.

**Green Tea — 2 calories per cup**
Contains catechins that may support metabolism. Drink unsweetened for virtually zero calories.

**Herbal Tea — 0-2 calories per cup**
Peppermint, chamomile, rooibos — all calorie-free when served without sugar or milk.

**Sparkling Water — 0 calories**
A satisfying alternative to sugary sodas with zero calories.

## How to Use Very Low Calorie Foods for Weight Loss

### Volume Eating

The strategy of "volume eating" centers on filling your plate with high-volume, low-calorie foods so you feel physically full without consuming excess calories. Add large portions of the foods listed above to every meal:

- Start meals with a large salad of mixed greens, [cucumber](/cucumber), [tomatoes](/tomatoes), and [bell peppers](/bell-peppers)
- Add extra vegetables to stir-fries, omelets, and soups
- Snack on raw [celery](/celery), [carrots](/carrots), and [cucumber](/cucumber) instead of chips or crackers
- Use [zucchini](/zucchini) noodles or [cauliflower](/cauliflower) rice to bulk up meals

### Smart Substitutions

Replace high-calorie foods with near-zero-calorie alternatives:

| Instead of | Try | Calories Saved |
|-----------|-----|----------------|
| Pasta (100g cooked, 131 cal) | [Zucchini](/zucchini) noodles (100g, 17 cal) | ~114 calories |
| White [rice](/rice) (100g cooked, 130 cal) | Cauliflower rice (100g, 25 cal) | ~105 calories |
| Tortilla wrap (150 cal) | [Lettuce](/lettuce) wrap (5 cal) | ~145 calories |
| Chips (150 cal per 30g) | [Cucumber](/cucumber) slices (8 cal per 50g) | ~142 calories |

### Stay Realistic

Very low calorie foods are tools, not complete meals. You still need adequate protein from foods like [chicken breast](/chicken-breast), [salmon](/salmon), and [eggs](/eggs), healthy fats from sources like [avocado](/avocado) and [almonds](/almonds), and complex carbohydrates from foods like [oats](/oats) and [sweet potato](/sweet-potato). Use near-zero-calorie foods to add volume and satisfaction around those nutrient-dense staples.

## Key Takeaways

- Truly zero calorie foods do not exist among whole foods — only water and unsweetened beverages qualify
- The "negative calorie food" concept is largely a myth, but many foods are so low in calories that the net impact is negligible
- The lowest-calorie whole foods include cucumber (15 cal/100g), lettuce (14 cal/100g), celery (16 cal/100g), and zucchini (17 cal/100g)
- Use these foods for "volume eating" — filling your plate with high-volume, low-calorie options to feel full on fewer total calories
- Very low calorie foods should complement a balanced diet that includes protein, healthy fats, and complex carbs, not replace it
