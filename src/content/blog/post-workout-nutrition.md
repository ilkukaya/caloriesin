## Introduction

What you eat after a workout directly influences how well your body recovers, adapts, and grows. During exercise, your muscles break down protein structures, deplete glycogen (stored carbohydrates), and sustain microscopic damage that needs to be repaired. The food you eat after training provides the raw materials for this repair process.

Getting post-workout nutrition right does not require supplements, special foods, or complicated timing protocols. It requires understanding three things: what to eat, how much, and roughly when. This guide covers all three based on current exercise science.

## The Three Post-Workout Priorities

### 1. Protein for Muscle Repair

During exercise, muscle protein breakdown increases. Consuming protein after your workout provides amino acids — particularly leucine — that stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS), the process by which your body builds new muscle tissue.

**How much:** 20-40g of protein within 2-3 hours after training. Research shows diminishing returns above 40g in a single sitting for most people.

**Best sources:**
- [Chicken breast](/chicken-breast) (150g) — 46g protein, 248 cal
- [Eggs](/eggs) (3 large) — 18g protein, 215 cal
- [Salmon](/salmon) (120g) — 24g protein, 250 cal
- Greek yogurt (200g) — 20g protein, 130 cal
- Whey protein shake — 24-30g protein, 100-130 cal
- [Tuna](/tuna) (120g canned) — 31g protein, 139 cal

### 2. Carbohydrates for Glycogen Replenishment

Your muscles store carbohydrates as glycogen, which fuels moderate-to-high intensity exercise. A typical weight training session depletes 30-40% of muscle glycogen, while endurance exercise can deplete 60-80% or more.

Consuming carbohydrates after exercise replenishes these stores, which is important for:
- Next-day training performance
- Preventing excessive muscle breakdown
- Supporting the immune system (which is temporarily suppressed post-exercise)

**How much:** 0.5-1.0g of carbohydrates per kg of body weight. More if your session was long or intense; less if it was a short strength session.

**Best sources:**
- [Rice](/rice) (150g cooked) — 39g carbs, 195 cal
- [Sweet potato](/sweet-potato) (200g) — 40g carbs, 172 cal
- [Banana](/banana) (1 medium) — 27g carbs, 105 cal
- [Oats](/oats) (50g dry) — 33g carbs, 190 cal
- [Quinoa](/quinoa) (150g cooked) — 30g carbs, 180 cal
- Whole grain bread (2 slices) — 26g carbs, 160 cal

### 3. Hydration and Electrolytes

You lose water and electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) through sweat during exercise. Rehydrating is critical for recovery. Dehydration of just 2% of body weight can impair performance, recovery, and cognitive function.

**How much:** Drink 500-750ml of water for every 0.5 kg of body weight lost during exercise. Most people should aim for at least 500ml within the first hour post-workout.

Foods rich in electrolytes:
- [Banana](/banana) — potassium
- [Sweet potato](/sweet-potato) — potassium
- [Spinach](/spinach) — magnesium
- [Avocado](/avocado) — potassium and magnesium
- Salt on your post-workout meal — sodium

## The Anabolic Window: What Science Actually Says

The old belief that you must eat protein within 30 minutes of your workout or "miss the window" is largely outdated. Research has clarified that:

- **If you ate a meal 2-3 hours before training,** those amino acids are still circulating and the urgency to eat immediately after is low. You have several hours.
- **If you trained fasted** (no food for 4+ hours), eating protein sooner (within 1-2 hours) is more important because your body has no recent amino acids to draw from.
- **Consistent daily protein intake** matters far more than precise post-workout timing. Someone eating 1.6-2.2g/kg of protein per day across 3-4 meals will build muscle regardless of whether they eat 15 minutes or 2 hours after training.

**Practical rule:** Eat a balanced meal containing protein and carbs within 2-3 hours of your workout. If that meal also happens to be close to your training, great. If it is 2 hours later because life happened, that is fine too.

## Post-Workout Meal Ideas

**Option 1: Chicken Rice Bowl (520 calories, 48g protein)**
- 150g [chicken breast](/chicken-breast), grilled — 248 cal
- 130g cooked [rice](/rice) — 169 cal
- 100g steamed [broccoli](/broccoli) — 34 cal
- Soy sauce and sesame seeds — 25 cal

**Option 2: Eggs and Toast (430 calories, 30g protein)**
- 3 large [eggs](/eggs), scrambled — 215 cal
- 2 slices whole grain toast — 160 cal
- 50g [spinach](/spinach), wilted — 12 cal

- 50g [avocado](/avocado) — 80 cal
- 50g [tomatoes](/tomatoes) — 9 cal
- [Lettuce](/lettuce) — 5 cal
- Mustard — 5 cal

### After Cardio/Endurance Training

Cardio-focused workouts deplete more glycogen, so emphasize carbohydrates more:

**Option 1: Salmon with Sweet Potato (480 calories, 30g protein)**
- 120g [salmon](/salmon), baked — 250 cal
- 200g [sweet potato](/sweet-potato), baked — 172 cal
- 100g [asparagus](/asparagus) — 20 cal

**Option 2: Protein Smoothie (420 calories, 35g protein)**
- 1 scoop whey protein — 120 cal
- 1 medium [banana](/banana) — 105 cal
- 100g [strawberries](/strawberries) — 32 cal
- 250ml milk — 122 cal
- 15g [oats](/oats) — 57 cal

**Option 3: Greek Yogurt Bowl (380 calories, 28g protein)**
- 200g nonfat Greek yogurt — 130 cal
- 1 medium [banana](/banana), sliced — 105 cal
- 30g granola — 132 cal

### Quick Post-Workout Snacks (When a Full Meal Isn't Possible)

Sometimes you cannot sit down for a proper meal right after training. These quick options hold you over until your next meal:

- [Apple](/apple) + 30g [peanut butter](/peanut-butter) — 272 cal, 8g protein

## Post-Workout Nutrition by Goal

### Muscle Building
- **Protein:** 30-40g
- **Carbs:** 40-60g
- **Fat:** Moderate (do not avoid, but do not load up)
- **Timing:** Within 2-3 hours
- **Example:** [Chicken breast](/chicken-breast) with [rice](/rice) and [broccoli](/broccoli)

### Fat Loss
- **Protein:** 30-40g (prioritize this)
- **Carbs:** 20-40g (slightly less to stay in deficit)
- **Fat:** Moderate
- **Timing:** Can delay if it fits your eating schedule (e.g., intermittent fasting)
- **Example:** [Eggs](/eggs) with vegetables and a small portion of [sweet potato](/sweet-potato)

### Endurance Training
- **Protein:** 20-30g
- **Carbs:** 60-100g (higher to replenish glycogen)
- **Fat:** Low (fat slows carb absorption when rapid glycogen replenishment matters)
- **Timing:** Within 1-2 hours for optimal glycogen recovery
- **Example:** [Rice](/rice) bowl with [chicken breast](/chicken-breast), vegetables, and a [banana](/banana)

## Foods to Avoid After a Workout

While no food is strictly "bad" after training, some are suboptimal:

- **High-fat fast food** — Fat slows digestion and delays protein and carb absorption when you want them delivered efficiently
- **Alcohol** — Impairs muscle protein synthesis by up to 37% and delays recovery
- **Sugary snacks with no protein** — A candy bar provides carbs but zero muscle-building protein
- **Large amounts of fiber** — While fiber is beneficial in general, very high-fiber meals post-workout can slow nutrient absorption when speed matters

## Key Takeaways

- Post-workout nutrition should prioritize protein (20-40g) and carbohydrates (30-60g)
- The "30-minute anabolic window" is mostly a myth — eat within 2-3 hours of training
- [Chicken breast](/chicken-breast), [eggs](/eggs), [salmon](/salmon), and Greek yogurt are the best whole food protein sources post-workout
- [Rice](/rice), [sweet potato](/sweet-potato), [banana](/banana), and [oats](/oats) are excellent carb sources for glycogen replenishment
- Daily total protein intake (1.6-2.2g/kg) matters more than precise meal timing
- Hydrate with at least 500ml of water and include electrolyte-rich foods in your post-workout meal
